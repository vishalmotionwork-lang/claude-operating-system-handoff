02

The core move: it flips between two modes

The whole video is built on one decision repeated over and over — which mode is this line? Narration and opinion go to the presenter. Anything concrete cuts away to a literal visual. It ping-pongs between the two roughly every 5–10 seconds.

Mode A Presenter

The host on camera, big word-by-word captions, no other graphics. Carries the voice — the hook, the opinion, the "here's the honest truth", the offer.

  • 1–3 words on screen at a time, synced to the voice
  • Bold white sans for normal words; serif italic for the one phrase that matters
  • Used when there's nothing concrete to show — it's the human talking

Mode B Proof

Cuts fully away from the host: b-roll, phone mockups, real documents, custom icons, screen-recordings, logos, counting numbers. Carries the claims — it shows the receipt for everything the voice asserts.

  • Every concrete noun, number, name or claim triggers a cut here
  • Always literal — show the actual thing, not a vibe
  • Snaps back to the presenter the moment the voice goes back to opinion
The one law underneath all of it

Show every noun. If the voice names a concrete thing — a product, a number, a company, a claim with a source — it appears on screen as a literal visual within about a third of a second. Nothing concrete is ever said without being shown. That single habit is what makes it feel dense and "high-production" rather than a talking head with captions.
